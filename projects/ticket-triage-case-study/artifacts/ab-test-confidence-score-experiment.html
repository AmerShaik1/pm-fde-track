Experiment Design & Results

Confidence Score → Segment 2 Trust

Testing whether showing a confidence score next to each AI draft increases accept-without-edit rate specifically for Segment 2 (solo operators), the group currently underperforming at 20%.
Concluded — Day 21 95% Confidence Level
Hypothesis
Written and locked before launch — this is what protects you from rationalizing whatever result shows up later.
If we show a confidence score (e.g. "92% match") next to each AI-drafted reply, then accept-without-edit rate for Segment 2 will increase, because part of their low trust may come from uncertainty about draft quality — not draft quality itself.
Experiment Setup
The structural decisions that make a result trustworthy rather than noise.
Population
Segment 2 merchants only
Isolated to the segment the hypothesis is actually about — testing on everyone would dilute any real effect.
Split
50 / 50, randomized
Random assignment, not "early sign-ups get treatment" — avoids confounding the change with who's more engaged.
Pre-committed sample size
~340 merchants / arm
Set upfront based on minimum detectable effect (8pt lift) — decided before seeing any data.
Duration / stopping rule
21 days, checked once
Not checked daily and stopped early — early "wins" regularly regress once more data comes in.
Control vs. Treatment
Control (A)
Current experience — AI draft shown with no confidence indicator, same as production today.
Treatment (B)
Identical draft, plus a visible confidence score (e.g. "92% match to similar resolved tickets") shown above the draft text.
Metrics Tracked
A real test watches more than the one number you're hoping moves.
Primary
Accept-without-edit rate
The metric the hypothesis is actually about — did the confidence score change trust behavior?
Guardrail
Ticket reopen rate
Checks the score isn't making merchants overconfident in genuinely bad drafts.
Diagnostic
Edit distance (edited, not rejected)
If accept rate doesn't move much, tells you whether merchants are getting closer to trusting it.
Results
Checked once, at the pre-committed sample size and duration — not mid-flight.
✓
Treatment lifted Segment 2 accept-without-edit rate from 20% → 33% (+13pts, statistically significant at 95% confidence). Guardrail metric (reopen rate) stayed flat, meaning merchants aren't blindly trusting the score on bad drafts. Edit distance on remaining edited-not-rejected drafts also dropped, suggesting a genuine trust shift, not just a fluke on the primary metric. Partial support for the hypothesis — uncertainty about draft quality was a real factor, but the remaining gap to Segment 1's 68% suggests trust isn't fully explained by confidence-visibility alone; the ticket-type confound (damaged/lost tickets) from the earlier diagnosis likely still accounts for some of the residual gap.
What this test deliberately avoided: the team did not check results daily. A day-3 read showed only a +4pt lift — tempting to call it "not working" and kill the test early. Waiting for the pre-committed sample size revealed the real, larger effect. This is the peeking problem in practice, not just in theory.
